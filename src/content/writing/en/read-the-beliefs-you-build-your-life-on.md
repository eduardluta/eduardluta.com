Imagine that God has written you a long letter containing absolute truth. Then imagine being unwilling to read it, while arranging your whole life around what somebody else says it contains.

That is the thought experiment in this video. It is meant to create a pause, not to prove that a particular text is divine.

**If a belief is important enough to organise my life, it is important enough for me to examine.**

## Borrowed certainty has a missing step

We learn through other people. Parents, teachers, religious leaders and friends can open doors we would never find alone. The problem is not receiving an interpretation. It is forgetting that it is an interpretation.

There is a difference between “I have read this passage” and “I have heard someone explain it.” Both can be useful. They are not the same kind of contact with the source.

A quotation without the paragraphs around it can leave out the question being answered. A translation can involve choices that a short social-media argument never mentions.

## Make the question concrete

Choose one claim that matters to you. Find the passage people cite for it. Read what comes before and after. Write down what the text says, what the explanation adds and what you still do not understand.

Then ask someone knowledgeable about the uncertain part. That is a more useful conversation than defending an entire identity before checking a sentence.

This is an editorial reading exercise, not a claim that expertise or tradition has no value. First-hand reading and informed guidance can strengthen each other.

My [question about God and Albanian](/writing/does-god-understand-albanian/) comes from the same desire to understand the words, not only repeat them. And freedom of conscience includes the space to investigate and change a belief. [¹](#article-notes-heading)

Do not let the importance of a text become the reason you never open it.
