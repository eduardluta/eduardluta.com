I say in the video that a man reaches maturity when he no longer needs everyone else’s approval. Then I give it an Albanian twist: perhaps that moment is when he lets his wife cut his hair.

It is a joke. Twelve seconds, a familiar domestic situation, and a small challenge to the importance we give our own image.

**Can I be comfortable with the person closest to me without imagining an audience judging the result?** That is the question I hear underneath it.

## Trust in an ordinary moment

There is no scientific threshold where a haircut certifies emotional maturity. Nor does choosing a barber say anything bad about a marriage. The joke works because it makes a large idea small enough to see.

A haircut is visible. If it comes out differently than expected, you cannot hide behind an impressive explanation. There you are, with your head and the person you trusted.

That is a useful image for the distinction between trust and performance. One concerns how two people are with each other. The other concerns how they imagine looking to everybody else.

## Leave room to be ordinary

There is a kind of pressure in always trying to look like the man who has everything under control. It leaves very little room for an uneven haircut, a laugh or a moment that does not need defending.

My clip does not offer a formula for marriage. It pokes at that pressure. Sometimes an everyday joke says something a serious speech makes too complicated.

If you want the longer version of the same theme, [love can be simple](/writing/love-making-room-for-your-child/) explores everyday attention rather than a grand performance.

Maybe the haircut is fine. Maybe it is not. The world can survive both.
