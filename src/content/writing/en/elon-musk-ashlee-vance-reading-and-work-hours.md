The question above my head is simple: has a book completely changed my perspective on business?

My answer begins by pushing back. Reading one book does not automatically change your life or make you successful. I describe it as a path of development: a book can give me another way to look at a problem in my life or my business.[1]

Then I name one that affected me strongly: **Ashlee Vance’s biography of Elon Musk.**

Both things can be true. A book can shake something loose in me. I still have to decide what to do with it.

## The book I mean

For anyone looking for it, Penguin’s catalogue identifies Vance’s *Elon Musk: How the Billionaire CEO of SpaceX and Tesla is Shaping our Future*. Its listed Virgin Digital edition was published on **21 May 2015**, ISBN **9780753550663**.[2] That identifies a reference edition; the clip does not establish which language or edition I read.

What I talk about in the video is the mentality of going all in. I say the biography challenged my way of thinking and pushed me to change part of myself. That is my account of its effect on me, not a promise about what another reader will become.

There is a difference between being moved by someone’s approach and treating every part of their life as a model to copy. The part I am discussing is commitment to the work. I am not offering a verdict on everything Musk has done, or a complete review of the biography.

## The 100-hour comparison needs a correction

Near the end, I recall a comparison between working **100 hours a week** and **40 hours a week**, then suggest that three months could surpass another person’s year. I connect the thought to the biography from memory. I do not identify a page, so this should not be presented as a verified quotation from Vance or Musk.

The arithmetic deserves to stand on its own. Using **13 weeks as an explicit approximation for three months**, and **52 weeks for a year**:

- 13 × 100 = **1,300 hours**.
- 52 × 40 = **2,080 hours**.

That is **780 fewer hours**, or **62.5%** of the annual total. The same number of hours would take **20.8 weeks** at 100 hours a week. Those assumptions do not support the three-month claim.[3]

Over the same period, 100 divided by 40 is **2.5**. That compares hours. It does not measure useful output, the quality of decisions or the value of what was made. My remark about momentum in the video is an interpretation, not measured evidence that one person can produce years of someone else’s work.

A bigger number on a timesheet cannot, by itself, prove a better result. This example is not a work schedule I am recommending.

## Keep the challenge; examine the claim

The useful question for me is what the book made me notice. Was I treating a problem as fixed? Was I asking for a result while avoiding the work it required? Those are questions I can examine without turning a remembered line into a formula for success.

An idea becomes more useful when I can make it concrete. I would take one problem, identify the assumption the passage challenged, and choose one change I can actually examine afterwards. That is an editorial way of working with a book, not a method the clip proves will improve performance.

It connects with [reading the beliefs I build my life on](/writing/read-the-beliefs-you-build-your-life-on/): the source, my interpretation and what I do next deserve separate attention. And [learning through a real AI project](/writing/how-to-start-learning-ai-with-a-real-project/) brings the same question back to something I can try, inspect and improve.

The answer to the viewer is still Ashlee Vance’s biography. What stayed with me was a different sense of commitment. Keeping that personal meaning does not require keeping a numerical shortcut that fails its own calculation.

**The book can open the perspective. I still have to live the answer.**
