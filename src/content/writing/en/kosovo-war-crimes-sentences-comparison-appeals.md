I reacted strongly to the judgment against the former KLA leaders. In the video I call it an injustice, compare prison terms and argue that the appeal process matters. That is my reaction and my position; it is not a substitute for the court's findings.

The central numerical comparison deserves to be checkable. The figures **109 years and 107 years can be reconstructed from the selected defendants below**. But their sum cannot, by itself, establish proportionality, collective guilt or the correctness of a judgment.

This article records the position checked on 23 September 2026. It concerns named people and specified cases, not a legal verdict on entire peoples.

## Start with the four sentences

The Kosovo Specialist Chambers' official case record reports that on **16 September 2026**, the Trial Panel convicted Hashim Thaçi, Kadri Veseli, Rexhep Selimi and Jakup Krasniqi of war crimes involving arbitrary detention, cruel treatment, torture and murder. The sentences were 25, 18, 13 and 25 years respectively. These are trial-level sentences. [Official case status](https://www.scp-ks.org/en/cases/end-trial).

| Defendant | Sentence used | Stage |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Hashim Thaçi | 25 years | Trial judgment, 2026 |
| Kadri Veseli | 18 years | Trial judgment, 2026 |
| Rexhep Selimi | 13 years | Trial judgment, 2026 |
| Jakup Krasniqi | 25 years | Trial judgment, 2026 |
| Salih Mustafa | 15 years | New sentence, 2024 |
| Pjetër Shala | 13 years | Appeal judgment, 2025 |

The first four add to **81 years**. Adding Mustafa's 15-year sentence and Shala's 13-year appeal sentence gives **109**. Their individual case histories are available from the KSC: [Mustafa](https://www.scp-ks.org/en/cases/salih-mustafa) and [Shala](https://www.scp-ks.org/en/cases/pjeter-shala). This deliberately makes visible that the selection mixes procedural stages.

## Reconstruct the 107-year side too

The comparison on the other side uses these six named Serbian/Yugoslav officials, rather than “all sentences for Serbia.” Five are documented in the ICTY's [Šainović and others case sheet](https://www.icty.org/x/cases/milutinovic/cis/en/cis_sainovic_al_en.pdf); Đorđević's term is in the [appeal judgment summary](https://www.icty.org/x/cases/djordjevic/acjug/en/140127-summary.pdf).

| Defendant | Sentence used |
| --- | ---: |
| Nikola Šainović | 18 years |
| Nebojša Pavković | 22 years |
| Vladimir Lazarević | 14 years |
| Sreten Lukić | 20 years |
| Dragoljub Ojdanić | 15 years |
| Vlastimir Đorđević | 18 years |

`18 + 22 + 14 + 20 + 15 + 18 = 107`.

The difference between these selected totals is **two years**, or about **1.9% of 107**. Each group has six people. The arithmetic mean is 18.17 versus 17.83 years, a difference of one-third of a year. These are descriptive calculations, not estimates of actual time remaining in custody or a ranking of the harm committed.

## Where the comparison stops working

The totals are striking. They also erase exactly the details a legal comparison needs: the offences proved against each person, the form of responsibility, aggravating and mitigating circumstances, and what changed on appeal.

**A sentence total is not an exchange rate for victims or for a country's suffering.** Nor should trial sentences on one side silently be presented as though they have the same procedural status as appeal outcomes on the other.

The wider wartime casualty, displacement, damage and force-size figures mentioned in the clip require separate datasets and definitions. They are not used as denominators here. Dividing years by a national death toll would not establish an individual's responsibility or an appropriate sentence.

For me, the stronger question is which specific finding or sentencing principle should be challenged, and on what evidence. That is harder work than comparing two bars. It is also the work a serious argument deserves.

## Why I mention an appeal

The example in the video is Ante Gotovina. The ICTY Appeals Chamber reversed his convictions and entered acquittals in its **16 November 2012** judgment. That is a documented outcome in a different case, not a prediction of the outcome here. [Official appeal judgment](https://www.icty.org/x/cases/gotovina/acjug/en/121116_judgement.pdf).

I want scrutiny and a fair appeal process. Recognising the victims' suffering does not require abandoning that demand. Demanding scrutiny does not require pretending the convictions were never entered.

The same discipline matters when discussing [public safety](/writing/kosovo-speed-cameras-road-safety/) or [education](/writing/kosovo-pisa-2025-learning-responsibility/): a number should clarify the argument, not replace the evidence.

If I ask for justice, I also have to ask my own argument to withstand scrutiny.
