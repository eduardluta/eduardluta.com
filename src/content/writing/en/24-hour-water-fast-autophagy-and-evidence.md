At 24 hours, I record an enthusiastic update. I say I feel very good, talk about exercise and describe having more mental space. Then I compare fasting with taking a car for a service.

**That comparison overstates what the video can show. Feeling well does not establish that damaged cells have been removed, an illness has been treated or a prolonged fast is safe.** The clip is a personal account, not a clinical assessment.

## Autophagy is real; the simple timer is misleading

Autophagy is a process in which cells break down and recycle their own components. The Nobel Assembly’s account of the research explains that it also operates at baseline, rather than existing only after a fasting threshold.[1]

So the video’s suggestion of a universal switch at a particular hour is too simple. A clock cannot tell me the level of autophagy in different tissues. Nor does the existence of this process make my “body service” analogy a demonstrated treatment.

## What a human fasting study actually measured

A 2024 *Nature Metabolism* study followed **12 volunteers during a seven-day water-only fast**, measuring changes in roughly **3,000 blood proteins**.[2] That is a small study of biological responses. It is not a trial demonstrating that my 100-hour target cures disease, or a measurement of whole-body autophagy in this video.

The distinction matters because a detected change is not automatically a benefit. To support a treatment claim, we would need evidence about meaningful health outcomes, who benefits, possible harms and how results compare with an appropriate alternative. My update supplies none of those comparisons.

## What I can say about the experience

I describe less time spent organising meals and more room for reflection. I estimate two or three hours freed up in my day. That is my estimate, without a time log; it is not an established productivity gain and certainly not a reason to skip nourishment for work.

I also talk about prayer, nature and feeling calmer. These are descriptions of my experience. The spiritual meaning does not need to be dressed up as a proven cellular mechanism to matter to me.

The risks I dismiss in the clip deserve a correction too. NCCIH lists fainting, weakness and dehydration among possible effects of fasting.[3] Confidence on camera does not cancel those possibilities. Discuss prolonged fasting with a qualified clinician; this series does not offer a safe fasting protocol.
