“I see you.” Three small words, and sometimes they carry more than a long compliment.

In the video, I am talking about the feeling that somebody recognises how you became who you are. The difficult parts, the humour, the perspective. They are interested in the person behind the polished introduction.

That recognition matters to me. It is also part of the thinking I connect, in the video, with dua.com and helping people meet a partner.

## A language carries more than information

You can translate the meaning of a sentence and still miss the memory inside it. A family expression can contain a whole room: who said it, how they laughed, what everyone understood without explanation.

In the video I mention celebrations and the familiarity of Albanian words. The point is not that every Albanian family celebrates in the same way. It is that shared references can give two people an immediate place to begin.

Culture is not a compatibility certificate. People who share a language can want very different lives. People from different backgrounds can understand each other deeply. Familiarity opens a conversation; it does not finish it.

## Being recognised still requires questions

Someone can understand a joke and still misunderstand your expectations about work, children, money or the future. Those things need talking about plainly.

Try moving from “we have the same values” to an example. What does responsibility look like during a hard month? How would we make a decision that affects both families? What does a respectful disagreement sound like to each of us?

These are conversation prompts, not a matching test. The answers matter because they describe the life people might build together, beyond the comfort of a familiar beginning.

## Why this connects to dua

I refer to dua.com in the video because this wish for connection is part of the purpose I associate with it. The platform currently presents itself as an Albanian dating app. This is a project I am connected to, not an independent product review.

The personal stories people share can be moving. They should not become a promise that an app will produce love, marriage or a particular outcome for everyone. A platform can create a chance to meet; the relationship belongs to the people who meet.

The phrase “I see you” also asks something of the person saying it. Am I listening, or have I simply recognised somebody who resembles me? Can I stay interested when the other person surprises me?

I want the recognition to go both ways. That is the part worth carrying from a first conversation into an ordinary day together. It is close to what I mean by [loving more and judging less](/writing/love-more-judge-less/).
