In this video I describe a decision that brought me a sense of peace: to stop lying and to keep the promises I make. The route began with a friend reading Ray Dalio and bringing those ideas into our conversations.

The change was not immediate. I describe it as something that took roughly two years to begin feeling integrated into my own life. That is my account, not a timetable someone else has to meet.

The example I give is ordinary. If a waiter returns too much change, I want to point it out and give the extra money back. There is no audience required. The action still matters when nobody would notice the alternative.

## What Dalio’s principle says—and what I am adding

Dalio’s own explanation of radical truth and transparency argues for making mistakes, weaknesses and reasoning visible at work so that people can examine them. He also acknowledges that the approach has disadvantages as well as advantages. [1]

My video takes the question into personal life: what happens when the story I tell other people is closer to the one I can honestly tell myself?

That does not turn a workplace philosophy into a rule requiring complete disclosure of every private thought. Privacy, another person’s confidence and the right to set a boundary still matter. A truthful answer can be that something is private or that you are not ready to discuss it.

## Separate the fact from the verdict

Honesty becomes more useful when I name what I know, what I think and what I am still unsure about. A strong opinion about somebody is not automatically a fact about them.

There is also a difference between saying something difficult and using truth as permission to be cruel. Timing, relevance and the other person’s dignity remain part of the conversation.

The internal work can be harder. Did I actually agree to something I could deliver, or did I want the relief of saying yes? Am I describing a setback accurately, or protecting an image of myself?

## Give a promise a shape

A promise is easier to keep when it contains an action and a time. Sending a draft by Friday is clearer than helping soon. If the situation changes, say so before the other person has to discover the gap.

The aim is not a perfect record. It is fewer places where another person has to guess whether your words still mean what they meant when you said them.

In the video I use an emphatic percentage while describing returning to the truth. It is conversational emphasis, not a measured honesty rate. The meaningful evidence would be the ordinary actions someone can experience over time.

That connects with [feedback between friends](/writing/good-friends-feedback-and-mutual-growth/): sincerity needs both the courage to speak and the willingness to hear an answer.

Peace, for me, is having fewer versions of the story to carry.
