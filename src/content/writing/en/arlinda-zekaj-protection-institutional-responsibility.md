She asked for protection. Why are we discussing which door she knocked on?

In the parliamentary excerpt inside my video, Donika Gërvalla says: “Unfortunately, Arlinda did not approach the Government.” My reaction is to that sentence and the distinction it makes.[1]

**A woman asking to stay alive should not need a personal route to political power.**

The video is angry because the subject deserves seriousness. Arlinda is not an argument to win against another party. A request for protection is a responsibility to act on.

## Keep the words and the evidence together

The clip includes Gërvalla saying that Arlinda approached the justice system and did not receive the protection she sought. She then suggests that approaching the Government might have brought engagement, while respecting the constitutional order. That conditional point should stay with the sentence I criticise.

Kosovo’s Assembly identifies Donika Gërvalla as justice minister in its account of the extraordinary session of **26 October 2026**.[3] The Prime Minister’s published speech that day names **Arlinda Sylaj Zekaj**, says she was killed the previous day and had approached the Prosecution three days before her death. It calls for an investigation into possible institutional negligence or inaction.[2]

That is official context for the case. It is not a completed finding against a particular official. Determining who received which warning, what they knew and what they did requires the records and a proper investigation.

Another inset shows people in the chamber appearing to smile. I find the contrast with this subject upsetting. The short edit does not establish what they were reacting to or what they intended. I should not turn my reaction to an expression into a factual accusation about their motive.

The stronger criticism does not need that guess: **the public discussion should return to the woman who asked for protection.**

## Institutional boundaries cannot become a maze for the victim

There is a real difference between demanding an answer from a minister and asking a minister to control an individual court decision. I want accountability without political interference in justice.

But respecting different responsibilities cannot mean sending the victim from one institution to another until nobody owns the risk. My question is not who can take credit after a tragedy. It is how protection reaches a person before it is too late.

The government’s **Regulation 01/2025** calls for victim-centred, non-discriminatory local coordination and direct cooperation in specific emergencies.[4]

This framework does not prove how Arlinda’s case was handled. It gives the public a concrete standard to ask about: who coordinated, what protective action followed the warning and where the response broke down, if it did.

I do not want a woman’s safety to depend on knowing a minister, belonging to a party or finding the politically convenient door. Protection must be organised around the person in danger.

## The call in my post has a date

My **27 October post** announced a protest for **Wednesday, 28 October 2026, at 16:00 in Zahir Pajaziti Square, Prishtina**.[1] That is the announcement carried by the source video and caption. This article does not report that the protest has already taken place or establish its attendance.

The point goes beyond one afternoon. Ask for the investigation. Ask for the records. Ask what changes for the next woman who reports danger. The same insistence on [a real answer to a public question](/writing/ambrozia-meta-follow-up-question-public-accountability/) has to survive the party argument.

I do not need the next victim to be someone from my own family before her life matters.

**She asked for protection. The answer should have been protection.**
