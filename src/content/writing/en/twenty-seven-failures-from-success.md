What if you were twenty-seven failures away from the biggest success of your life? Imagine how differently you might wake up tomorrow.

That is the whole provocation in this short video. The number makes an uncomfortable possibility visible: perhaps what I experience as rejection could sometimes be part of learning how to do the thing.

**Twenty-seven is not a prediction. Nobody has given us the map.**

## The number changes the story, not the odds

If every unsuccessful attempt feels like a verdict on who I am, beginning becomes expensive before I have even tried. The thought experiment changes the meaning of the attempt: what if this experience could teach me something?

That does not make every failure useful. Repeating the same action without examining the result can simply repeat the cost. Some projects should change direction. Some should stop.

The distinction is between collecting disappointments and extracting information.

## Give the next attempt a question

Before trying again, write down what you are testing. Afterwards, separate what happened from the explanation you prefer. Then decide what to change.

For example, if nobody responds to a proposal, “I am useless” is not a useful description of the result. “I sent this offer to five people and received no replies” is something you can examine. You might review the offer, the audience or how clearly you explained the next step. That is an invented example, not a claim about my own results.

The approach is small enough to use without pretending the ending is guaranteed. You are allowed to be disappointed. You are also allowed to learn and make another choice.

My [video about trying and doing](/writing/trying-doing-visible-progress/) asks for visible action. This one asks what meaning we give the outcome when the action does not work.

Do not count failures as a promise. Look for what the next attempt can teach you.
