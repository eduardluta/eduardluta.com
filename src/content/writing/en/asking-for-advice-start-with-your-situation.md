People send me messages asking what they should do with their lives. I appreciate the trust. But sometimes the question is so large that I cannot find a useful place to begin.

In the video, my response is blunt: what are you already doing to help yourself? I mention reading, writing, learning, exercise and making something. The useful part of that question is the information it can uncover—not a test of whether somebody deserves help.

Nobody has to earn support by becoming productive first. Being stuck can itself be the reason a person needs help. A stranger reading a message cannot diagnose the cause from a few sentences.

## Replace the enormous question with a concrete one

“What should I do with my life?” leaves almost everything unknown. What matters to you? Which responsibilities do you carry? What can you afford in time and money? What is the immediate obstacle?

A better starting point might be: “I want to change jobs. I have revised my CV and sent five applications, but I am getting no interviews. Could you look at how I describe my experience?” Those numbers are an illustrative example, not a story about a particular follower.

Now the other person can respond to something real. They might identify a weak application, suggest a different role or ask a question you missed. Their advice is still advice, not a guarantee.

## Trying something is also a way to find the obstacle

You do not need an impressive achievement to report. “I tried to study for ten minutes and could not concentrate” contains more useful information than pretending everything is fine. “I do not know where to start” is also a valid starting point, especially when followed by the constraints you face.

The smallest step might be opening the document, arranging childcare for one hour, asking a teacher for clarification or booking appropriate professional support. The step should fit the problem. A workout is not an answer to every financial, health or relationship difficulty.

## Be specific about the help you want

Would you like feedback, an introduction, information, someone to listen or practical assistance? These are different requests. Naming the request gives the other person a fair chance to help—or to say honestly that they cannot.

I also owe people that honesty. Running a business or sharing reflections online does not qualify me to solve every problem. Sometimes the useful response is to point someone towards a person with the relevant knowledge.

My underlying invitation is to participate in the next step, at the scale that is possible for you. It connects to the distinction between [trying and visible progress](/writing/trying-doing-visible-progress/). The aim is a clearer starting point, not a judgement about your worth.
