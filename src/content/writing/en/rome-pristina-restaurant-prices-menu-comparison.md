The video is a joke after a lunch I enjoyed. We were in Rome, and apparently we had finally found somewhere more expensive than Pristina.

A joke takes nine seconds. A useful price comparison needs a little more care. The receipt in the clip does not give this article a legible, complete bill to analyse. It would be wrong to fill in the total or guess how many people and dishes it covered.

So here is a separate, checkable example: two restaurants’ own published menus. Neither is identified here as the restaurant from my video.

## €4.50 and €13: what are we comparing?

HOME Restaurant & Lounge Bar in Pristina lists Penne Carbonara at **€4.50**. Antica Osteria di Roma lists Carbonara at **€13**. Their online menus were checked on 16 October 2026.[1][2]

| Published menu item | Restaurant | Listed price |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Penne Carbonara | HOME, Pristina | €4.50 |
| Carbonara | Antica Osteria di Roma, Rome | €13.00 |

The difference is **€8.50**. Dividing €13 by €4.50 gives **2.89 times the price**, rounded to two decimals. Expressed as an increase relative to the Pristina example, that is **188.9% more**.

Those last two figures describe the same gap in different ways. A price that is 2.89 times another price is about 189% higher, not 289% higher.

## The name is similar; the dishes are different

HOME describes a version with dried beef and cream. The Rome menu uses guanciale and pecorino. These are different recipes; the sources do not establish equal portion sizes.[1][2]

That matters. This is a comparison of two selected menu prices, not an identical product in two shops. It also excludes drinks, any separately charged extras and the rest of a meal. Online listings can lag behind the menu handed to you at the table.

We cannot turn two dishes into an average for two cities. Nor can a euro price alone show how affordable lunch is for someone earning a local salary. In the [Kosovo–Germany grocery comparison](/writing/kosovo-germany-grocery-prices/), prices and income are deliberately separate parts of the calculation.

## Keep the joke, check the claim

What I can say from the clip is simple: I enjoyed lunch and made a joke about the price. What these menus add is a concrete example with visible inputs and arithmetic anyone can repeat.

If you want to compare your own two meals, keep the receipt and note the number of people, dishes, drinks and extra charges. Then decide whether you are comparing one plate, a whole meal or a price relative to income. Each answers a different question.
