We arrived in Hamburg, and the campsite immediately gave me something to talk about. The nature, the space, the infrastructure. In the video I am excited to get into the city and meet people.

That is the part of travelling I care about most. A place becomes different when someone there has a story to tell you. On this trip, I want to meet people who use dua, hear what works, hear what does not, and meet people whose lives crossed through it.

An invitation is easy to say into a camera. Making a meeting easy to reach takes a little more thought.

## Leave the camper, understand the ticket

For an ordinary Hamburg AB journey, HVV lists an adult single at **€4.10**, an adult day ticket at **€8.20**, and a group day ticket at **€16.40**. The group ticket covers up to five people travelling together. These are the published full fares checked on 23 September 2026; digital discounts are not included in the comparisons below. [HVV fares and conditions](https://www.hvv.de/en/tickets/single-day-tickets).

I have not established the campsite's fare zone here. Check the complete journey from your actual stop: an AB ticket is useful only if the journey fits its coverage.

The arithmetic for one adult is straightforward. Two €4.10 singles cost the same as the €8.20 day ticket. A third single brings the total to **€12.30**, so the day ticket saves **€4.10**, or **33.3%**, against three full-price singles. Short-distance fares and existing passes can change that decision.

For three adults making several journeys together, three individual day tickets cost **€24.60**. One group ticket costs **€16.40**: **€8.20 less**. For five adults, it is €41 versus €16.40, a **€24.60 saving**. This is a ticket comparison, not a claim about what our group bought.

The condition matters as much as the price. If everybody goes home separately, a shared group ticket may not fit the plan. An individual ticket buys a different kind of flexibility.

## The harbour can be part of the journey

Hamburg's public ferries make the water part of its transport network. HADAG's route 62 links Landungsbrücken with Finkenwerder; check its current stops and departures before travelling. It gives a practical reason to look beyond the car window. [Official route 62 information](https://hadag.de/en/routes/62/).

That is a possible outing, not a route I am claiming to have completed in the video. The clip catches the arrival and the anticipation. The city visit is still ahead of it.

## Give a meetup a shape people can actually use

An open invitation needs a public meeting point, an agreed time, and a clear way to recognise the group. It also needs room for people who do not know anyone yet.

For a future meeting, I would keep the first part simple: a place reachable by public transport, enough time to arrive without rushing, and a short introduction before people split into conversations. A drink or a walk can follow. Nobody should have to solve a complicated itinerary just to say hello.

This article is not a dated event announcement. The video invites people to connect; it does not establish a confirmed venue or booking.

## The stories I want to hear

There is a difference between seeing a number in a product dashboard and meeting the person behind it. Did the conversation turn into a relationship? Where did the app feel awkward? What almost made someone leave? Specific answers are much more useful than polite compliments.

I explore the size of that wider community in [Albanians in Germany](/writing/albanians-in-germany-beyond-the-numbers/). The statistics need careful definitions. The people need time and attention.

The campsite gives us somewhere to stop. Meeting people gives the journey a reason.
