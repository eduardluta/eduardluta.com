A car stops beside a red carpet. King Carl XVI Gustaf approaches Volodymyr Zelensky, who opens his arms. The Albanian overlays in the clip I shared turn those seconds into two claims: a refused handshake, then spitting.[1]

The wider record does not support that account. **Handshakes are documented. The claim about spitting remains unverified.**[2][3]

My caption called this a moment that speaks for itself. Here, the wider record needs to speak too.

## The handshake outside the frame

This is footage from the King’s visit to Lviv on 17 April 2026, not a newly recorded October meeting. The Swedish Royal Court identifies the meeting at Potocki Palace and publishes a photograph of the two men shaking hands indoors.[2]

SVT’s report includes the same arrival scene, then footage of handshakes indoors and in an outside courtyard. Its journalists were present during the visit. The report also carries the Royal Court’s response that the two men shook hands several times.[3]

That is concrete evidence against presenting the visit as a refusal to shake hands. It does not tell me what either man felt during each gesture outside.

## A movement is not proof of intent

The overlay about spitting is stronger than the evidence available here. A head or mouth movement in this short recording does not establish that someone spat, where anything landed or why they moved. I cannot turn that interpretation into a verified event.

The meeting’s documented subject also matters. Ukraine’s presidency records discussions about defence cooperation, humanitarian support and energy assistance.[4] A few gestures cannot replace that record of the visit.

I shared the video on 22 October. The scene belongs to April. Keeping those dates separate is part of describing it honestly.

This is the same distinction I made in [the edited protest clip](/writing/speech-detention-and-a-protest-clip/): a caption can connect images into a story before the images have established it.

**The frame is real. The conclusion still needs evidence.**
