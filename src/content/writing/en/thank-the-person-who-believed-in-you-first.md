This video is a thank-you to the person who hears the first version. The uncertain idea. The thought you cannot explain cleanly yet. The thing you would not say in a room full of people whose judgement you fear.

That person might be a friend, a sibling, a parent or your partner. In the recording I describe them as someone who can believe in us before we manage to hold that belief ourselves.

The business language for it is the first follower. The human part is having somebody beside you when there is very little evidence to show.

## The idea behind the phrase

Derek Sivers’s 2010 talk uses footage of a dancing crowd to argue that the first person who joins makes participation visible to others. His emphasis includes treating early followers as equals, rather than making the moment entirely about the leader. [1]

It is a useful lens for the video. It is not proof that one supporter determines every venture’s outcome. Plenty of ideas with loyal support still fail; plenty change because somebody asks a difficult question.

What I want to hold on to is the contribution that is easy to lose from the finished story. Once there is a result, we can forget who was present before it looked convincing.

## Support can contain disagreement

The first person who takes you seriously does not have to endorse every version of your plan. Sometimes taking it seriously means asking where it breaks, what you have missed or whether you are ready to do the work.

That is different from making you feel foolish for caring. There is a form of criticism that keeps the person intact while challenging the idea.

If I want that kind of support, I also have a responsibility to receive it. Asking for honesty and punishing an honest answer makes the invitation impossible.

## Make the thank-you specific

A useful thank-you names the action. You listened when I could not explain it. You helped me test the first version. You told me what needed to improve. These are examples of what someone might say, not quotations from an exchange in the video.

Naming the action gives the other person a place in the story beyond being described as supportive. It says that their time and attention registered.

There is no need to wait for a public success to do this. The relationship matters even if the project never becomes what you hoped. A person is not valuable only because they helped produce a result.

I return to the business side in [one believer and a real test](/writing/first-supporter-and-a-real-business-test/). Here the invitation is simpler: think of the person who has heard your unfinished thoughts, and tell them what that has meant.

Do not let the finished story erase the person who was there at the beginning.
