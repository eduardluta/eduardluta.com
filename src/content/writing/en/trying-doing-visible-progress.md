I use my glasses to make a point in this video. Saying that I am trying to take them off does not remove them. At some point, the action has to happen.

It is a deliberately blunt lesson from business: **a description of effort is not the same thing as the result.**

But the glasses are a small action. Building a company, learning a skill or changing a difficult situation is more complicated. An honest article needs that distinction.

## Make the next step visible

“I am trying to build something” can cover many different situations. Perhaps I have made a prototype. Perhaps I am researching a specific obstacle. Perhaps I have spent another week imagining the finished product.

Those are not identical. Giving the next action a concrete form makes the difference easier to see.

| Vague statement | A visible next action |
| --- | --- |
| I am trying to learn | Complete one exercise and check the mistake |
| I am trying to sell | Send one clear proposal to a relevant person |
| I am trying to build | Make one small part work and demonstrate it |

These are illustrative examples, not a report of my own business metrics.

## Effort can be real before success appears

The clip’s binary language is a push toward action. It should not erase genuine work that has not yet produced an outcome. Research, recovery, practice and long projects can contain uncertainty that removing glasses does not.

A useful distinction is between an outcome I cannot fully control and a next step I can take. I can submit a proposal; I cannot force acceptance. I can practise; I cannot promise mastery by a particular date.

That is why [the thought experiment about 27 failures](/writing/twenty-seven-failures-from-success/) belongs beside this video. Action makes learning possible. An unsuccessful result still needs interpretation.

Before saying “I’m trying” again, name what happened today—and what the next action is.
