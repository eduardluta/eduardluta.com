Imagine spending three days with someone you love without speaking to them. In this video, I remember doing that with my wife roughly four years earlier. We rented a villa and made our own agreement: no talking, no phone, no television and no reading. We allowed ourselves to meditate and write.

The arithmetic is simple: **72 hours ÷ 24 = three days**. What occupied those days is harder to summarise. With less new material coming in, I had more time with the thoughts, ideas and unresolved things already there.

## Our retreat was a private experiment

The caption calls it a “Vipassana experience.” That needs a distinction. This was the arrangement I describe with my wife, not a documented completion of an official course.

In S. N. Goenka’s tradition, the introductory course is a structured ten-day programme with teachers. Its discipline includes silence and no reading or writing.[1] Our three days together, with writing allowed, should not be presented as the same format.

| Detail | Our arrangement in the video | Goenka-tradition introductory course |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Duration | Three days | Ten days |
| Writing | We allowed it | Not permitted |
| Setting | A villa we rented | An organised course |

This comparison identifies two different arrangements. It does not score their spiritual value or suggest that duration measures insight.

## The experience we compared afterwards

I describe something that still gives me goosebumps: when we spoke again, my wife and I felt that our accounts of an inner experience matched. We interpreted it as a connection to an earlier life in India.

That is how I describe and understand the experience. **The video cannot establish a past life, telepathy or an independently verified match between two accounts.** It contains my later recollection, not separately recorded observations from the retreat. The feeling of connection can be meaningful without turning it into a scientific finding.

What I return to at the end of the clip is the time I had to sit with myself. No performance, no fresh distraction, no conversation to keep moving away from the uncomfortable part.

## Keep room for support

A positive memory is not a universal retreat recommendation. NCCIH notes that difficult experiences can occur with meditation and advises against using it to replace or delay medical care.[2] Silence should not become a rule that stops someone asking for help.

For me, the lasting question is smaller than the extraordinary story: what do I notice when I stop filling every gap? It connects with my [reflection on *Autobiography of a Yogi*](/writing/autobiography-of-a-yogi-self-forgiveness-and-responsibility/) and the space between an experience and the meaning I give it.
