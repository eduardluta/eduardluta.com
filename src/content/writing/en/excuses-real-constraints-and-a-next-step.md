A dark car interior. A screen. A short interview about excuses.

That is the scene in the video I shared. One person asks whether excuses ever help us. The other answers with a blunt warning about what we can lose when we allow them to direct our lives. Those are the interview speakers’ words; my caption adds my own challenge to stop letting excuses win.

The question I want to carry beyond that clip is practical: **what can I do with the next decision?** If something real blocks the way, name what is needed. If I am avoiding an available action, make that action specific enough to attempt.

## When an excuse hides the next step

Think of an unfinished project. “I need to prepare more” might be completely accurate. There may be a skill, a permission or a piece of information missing. But what, exactly?

If the answer is a particular question, I can write it down and decide whom to ask. If the answer keeps changing every time I get close to starting, that is worth examining too. I might be protecting myself from producing a first version that somebody can judge.

## Real constraints deserve a real response

Illness, caring responsibilities, missing money or an unsafe situation cannot be wished away with a motivational line. Sometimes the appropriate next move is asking for help, changing the scale of a task or choosing to wait.

These editorial prompts allow for a real barrier and avoidance to exist together.

Try finishing this sentence: **“The thing preventing me from starting is ___; the support or decision I need is ___.”** If you cannot fill the second part yet, finding it can be the next task.

## Give an intention a place to happen

A 2006 meta-analysis by Peter M. Gollwitzer and Paschal Sheeran combined 94 independent tests and found that plans linking a situation to an action improved goal attainment overall.[1] That supports trying a specific plan; it does not promise an individual result.

Here is an example developed for this article. Suppose you want to write something but keep reorganising your notes. At the next planned writing session, open one document and write its first paragraph. Afterwards, note what made starting easier or what still needs attention.

Afterwards, describe what happened and adjust the task or seek support. A missed attempt can inform the next decision.

## Bring the question back to your own project

This connects with [learning AI through a real project](/writing/how-to-start-learning-ai-with-a-real-project/): choose a task whose result you can inspect. It also connects with [finding my own voice by starting to post](/writing/why-i-started-posting-finding-my-own-voice/). A first attempt makes the next question more concrete.

You can leave the clip with a strong feeling and still leave your project untouched. Before closing this page, name the thing you want to move forward, the barrier that matters and the next action or support you will seek.

Give the next decision something real to work with.
