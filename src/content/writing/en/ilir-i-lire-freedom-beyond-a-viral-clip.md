A few seconds of a street scene. Helmeted figures in dark uniforms move around a corner; several fall, and the sequence repeats. An arrow and Albanian meme text turn the moment into a joke about young people.

My caption took that small clip somewhere much bigger: **Ilir. I lirë.** Freedom, identity and the refusal to surrender your spirit.

There is a limit to what the footage tells us. Its original location, date and wider circumstances have not been established here. An Albanian caption does not make a recording evidence of an event in Albania.

## What I meant by freedom in our DNA

The caption plays with the sound of *Ilir* and *i lirë*, meaning free. It is wordplay, not proof of a word’s historical origin.

I also wrote about freedom being in our blood, in our DNA. That is a metaphor for resilience and attachment to freedom. It does not establish a biological trait shared by an ethnic group.

The conviction behind it is still strong: control over a place does not automatically give someone control over the people’s convictions. That was the thought I attached to the clip.

It is close to what drew me to the scene from [Nëntori i dytë about dignity and orders](/writing/nentori-i-dyte-besa-dignity-and-orders/). Power can make a demand. A person still has to live with the meaning of obeying it.

## Give freedom a practical meaning

A national feeling becomes more useful when it reaches everyday life. Can someone speak without losing their dignity? Can people gather peacefully? Does the law treat them equally even when their views differ?

Albania’s Constitution provides concrete references for those questions: equality before the law, freedom of expression and peaceful, unarmed assembly. [1]

These provisions are context for the Albanian public conversation in my caption. They are not a legal assessment of the unidentified street scene.

The people inside a viral clip can disappear behind its joke. The same thing happens when a protester becomes just a label. I wrote about [seeing the person behind a protest](/writing/work-dignity-person-behind-protest/) because dignity has to survive that reduction too.

An image can carry a feeling. It cannot carry the whole history or establish who was right in an incident we have not fully seen.

I wanted to express the stubbornness of wanting to be free. That idea deserves more than eleven seconds of attention.
