Working with love stories, I kept noticing something I wanted to talk about: people who were absorbed in building a life together seemed to be living inside a different conversation.

They were thinking about the person beside them, the future, the version of themselves they wanted to bring into that relationship. In this video I contrast that with the endless habit of finding something wrong with everybody else.

I put it provocatively: love more, judge less.

## An invitation, not a diagnosis

The video makes a broad link between being in love and judging people less. I would not turn that into a scientific rule. People in relationships can be cruel; people who are single can be generous. You cannot diagnose a stranger's capacity for love from one unpleasant comment.

The useful question is closer to home: where am I putting my own attention? Am I trying to understand something, improve something or care for someone? Or am I using another person's life as material for a performance of superiority?

That question is uncomfortable because it applies to me too.

## Criticism can have a job to do

Judging less does not mean pretending every action is harmless. A dishonest claim can need correcting. A boundary can need defending. A person affected by a decision can have a legitimate objection.

The distinction I want is between criticism that identifies a real issue and contempt that reduces a whole person to a label. “This number is wrong” gives us something to check. “You are worthless” gives us very little to build with.

It also matters whether I know enough. A short clip rarely contains a person's entire relationship, history or intention. Sometimes the most honest response is a question. Sometimes it is to leave the comment unwritten.

## Put some attention into the life you want

In the video, love extends beyond a romantic partner: love for life, for yourself, for the person close to you. I mean an active investment of attention, not a permanent good mood.

That can be ordinary. Ask a better question. Notice an effort. Finish something that makes another person's day easier. Spend time on the relationship instead of narrating everybody else's.

None of those actions earns immunity from criticism. They simply move the centre of the day towards something I value.

The feeling of being understood is part of this too; I explore it in [“I See You”](/writing/i-see-you-shared-language-and-connection/). I want to be seen with some generosity. It seems fair to practise giving that generosity back.
