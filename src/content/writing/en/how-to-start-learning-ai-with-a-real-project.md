Someone asks how to learn AI before finishing university. I do not want the answer to become another long speech that postpones the first attempt.

In the video I offer three ways in: use the providers' learning material, ask an assistant to help organise your learning, and build something. The third is the one that excites me most, because a project gives every new question somewhere to go.

You do not need a degree to begin using a conversational tool. Becoming a researcher or safely building a complex production system is a different level of work. Starting and mastering are different milestones.

## 1. Get a map of the basics

These official starting points were checked for this article:

| Resource | Useful starting point |
| --- | --- |
| [OpenAI's ChatGPT quickstart](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/quickstart) | Getting started with ChatGPT |
| [Google AI Essentials](https://grow.google/ai-essentials/) | Beginner course with practical activities; no prior experience required |
| [Claude Academy](https://academy.claude.com/) | Anthropic's learning resources, including AI Fluency |

Pick one that fits your immediate task. Check current access, language and any price before enrolling. A completion certificate records a course; it is not proof that you can solve every AI problem. The video's rough learning-time estimate and user-count claim are not benchmarks used here.

## 2. Make the practice fit your life

Here is a prompt I would use as an exercise:

> I want to make a simple information page for a small service business. I know the business but I am new to building websites. I can practise for 30 minutes at a time. Give me a short plan in plain language, one task per session, and a way to check each result. Ask for missing information instead of inventing it.

Replace the business and time with your own circumstances. Check any links the assistant suggests. If a step assumes knowledge you do not have, ask it to break the step down. A learning plan is useful when you can actually perform the next action.

## 3. Build something small enough to finish

For that example page, write the real service, audience, opening hours and contact route first. Then ask for a structure and a first version. Review it on a phone. Follow every link. Confirm that it has not invented testimonials, prices or qualifications. Use fictional contact details while experimenting.

An illustrative first week could be four 30-minute sessions: define the task, create the first version, check it, improve it. That is **4 × 30 = 120 minutes**, or two hours of scheduled practice. It is a time budget, not a promise that you will finish a website or master AI in two hours.

For a public site, learning also continues through hosting, accessibility, privacy and maintenance. A convincing preview does not finish those jobs.

My [reflection on AI and expertise](/writing/ai-opportunity-curiosity-practice-and-real-expertise/) makes the same distinction: curiosity starts the work; evidence of what you can do comes from checking it.

Start with one result you can explain. Then make the next one better.
