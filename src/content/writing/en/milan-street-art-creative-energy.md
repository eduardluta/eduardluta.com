It is evening in Milan. The camera rests on a mural: an older woman with her face against her hand, words painted beside her, people moving above a railing.

In the video, I say that every city has its own energy and personality. Milan feels creative, expressive and full of things that make me want to create.

At that moment, it is my favourite city of the trip. That is a personal response to being there, rather than a verdict every visitor needs to share.

## When the street becomes part of the art

What I like about this scene is how little separation there is between the artwork and everyday life. People are passing. Evening lighting falls across the paint. The wall belongs to the view, alongside everything happening around it.

You can encounter a piece like this without first deciding to spend an afternoon looking at art.

That is the feeling behind the clip. Somebody has made something visible, and seeing it makes me want to make something too.

## Milan makes room for street art

There is practical context behind the city's public art. Milan's municipality lists **100 designated “Muri liberi” walls** available for street artists, with a map showing their locations. Its guidance says these particular walls can be painted without requesting authorisation or paying a fee.[1]

That permission belongs to the designated surfaces. It is not permission to paint on any wall in the city.

Milan also has a municipal office for art in public spaces, which inventories and promotes existing work and supports new projects.[2] These are useful places to begin if the city inspires you to explore further.

I have not established the artist or exact address of the mural in my video, or whether it belongs to either programme. The municipal information explains part of Milan's wider public-art setting.

## Bring the impulse home

In the caption, I connect the experience with creating and exploring ourselves. For me, the useful question is what to do with that feeling after the walk ends.

Start with the particular thing that caught your attention. Was it the colour, the expression, a line of text or the way the work sat in the street? Name it. Then make something small in response: a paragraph, a sketch, a photograph with a deliberate composition.

The result does not have to resemble the mural. What matters is giving the impulse somewhere to go.

I return to this in [my reflection on creativity and the inner voice](/writing/creativity-inner-voice-and-the-untethered-soul/): noticing what moves you is a beginning. Making something gives that attention a form.

Milan gave me that push. I want to keep it after leaving.
