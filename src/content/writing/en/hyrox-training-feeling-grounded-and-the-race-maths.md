I am in the gym with a friend, enthusiastic after a mixed training session. We talk about running, rowing, the ski machine and throwing a ball. I say I feel powerful and present again.

The opening addresses people who feel unsettled after a retreat or using substances. I recommend hard training in the energy of the moment. **That needs a limit: a workout is not a treatment for persistent confusion, hallucinations or other serious mental-health symptoms.** My good feeling after exercise does not establish that it would resolve somebody else’s distress.

## The useful part is concrete attention

There is something very ordinary in the scene: a friend, a task, a distance to cover. When I say I feel grounded, I mean that I feel present in what I am doing. That is a report about my state in the video, not a medical diagnosis or a guaranteed effect of intensity.

I also mention a twinge in my back on the ski machine. That detail matters alongside the enthusiasm. Technique, manageable effort and responding to pain belong in training; pushing harder is not always the useful next step.

## What a HYROX race contains

The official format combines **eight 1 km runs with eight workout stations**, alternating running and a station.[1] That gives **8 km of running**, with the station work on top. The three kilometres mentioned in our gym conversation describe that session, not a complete official race.

Here is a simple illustration using two hypothetical running paces:

| Assumed pace throughout the runs | Running distance | Running time only |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 5 minutes per kilometre | 8 km | 40 minutes |
| 6 minutes per kilometre | 8 km | 48 minutes |

The arithmetic is 8 × 5 = 40 and 8 × 6 = 48. An extra minute per kilometre adds eight minutes across the runs. **Neither result is a finishing-time prediction:** station work and transitions remain, and fatigue may change the running pace.

I say we have registered for Rome and have around 60 days ahead of us. This records what I said then. The video does not verify a race result, and the article does not turn that plan into an achievement that has already happened. Nor do the loads we mention in training establish the official requirements for any particular division.

## A training plan is not a substitute for care

Being unsettled after an experience and having symptoms of psychosis are not interchangeable. I am not diagnosing anyone in this clip. If someone is experiencing hallucinations or loss of contact with reality, the NHS advises seeking medical help immediately.[2] Trying to exhaust those symptoms away is not an adequate response.

For ordinary training, choose an activity and level that suit your condition, learn the movement and allow room for recovery. The part I want to carry forward from the video is enthusiasm for doing something real with another person. The same balance sits in my [advice about work and health](/writing/advice-at-24-ai-work-and-health/): ambition should include care for the person doing the work.
