Some people miss you. Some miss being able to control you.

That is the distinction I make in this short video. It comes from thinking about what happens when somebody starts to grow, change their habits or take their own life more seriously.

People who care can be glad for you. They may support the change, get curious about it, even join you in something new. Other reactions can feel like a demand to become the earlier version of yourself, because that version was more convenient.

The difficult part is telling the difference without pretending you can read another person’s mind.

## Look at the response, not the label

One disappointed comment does not prove a hidden desire to control you. A friend may genuinely miss your time together. A partner may have a reasonable concern. You can change in a way that is good for you and still need to explain how it affects somebody else.

**The useful question is what happens when you state a reasonable boundary clearly.** Is there room for discussion? Can the other person be disappointed and still respect your choice? Or does the same pattern repeatedly return: guilt, pressure, punishment for saying no?

Those are observations you can examine. “They are jealous of my growth” is an interpretation, and it may be wrong. Personal growth is not a shield against every uncomfortable piece of feedback.

## A boundary can leave room for connection

Imagine a friend who wants to meet every evening, while you are protecting time for a project. An example answer could be: “I cannot come tonight. I still want to see you. Can we choose another day?”

That is a made-up situation, not a conversation from my life. Its purpose is to separate affection from unlimited availability. You can care about someone without offering every hour they ask for.

The response also needs context. If you repeatedly cancel a plan you agreed to, the other person’s frustration may be about reliability. If you offer an alternative and they repeatedly refuse any arrangement that includes your needs, that is a different conversation.

In the video I acknowledge how painful this can be when it involves people we love deeply. Recognising that a relationship needs to change does not erase what it has meant. It does mean that affection alone cannot do all the work of mutual respect.

My [note about friendship and honest feedback](/writing/good-friends-feedback-and-mutual-growth/) belongs beside this one. Support is allowed to challenge you. It should also leave you room to remain a person with choices.

You can miss the old rhythm together without requiring the old version of me forever.
