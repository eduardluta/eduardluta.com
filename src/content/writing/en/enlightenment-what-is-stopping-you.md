The moon leaves a thin path across the water. Over that image, I put a direct sentence: your goal in this life is enlightenment—to meet your true spirit.

Then a question.

**What’s stopping you?**

I shared it as a spiritual conviction. I am inviting you into a question I care about, rather than reporting a discovery that every person must accept. For me, the question is about the distance between the life we keep performing and the part of ourselves we still want to meet honestly.

## What I mean by meeting your true spirit

We can become very good at explaining what we do. The work. The plans. The version of ourselves that makes sense to other people.

It is harder to ask what remains when we stop defending that version. What do I value when nobody is rewarding it? Where am I acting from fear? Which part of my life feels borrowed?

Those are questions, not evidence that I have already reached the end of the search. The word *enlightenment* can become another achievement to display. I do not want to turn an inner life into another ranking.

The word also has a history beyond my caption. In her account of Buddhism for The Metropolitan Museum of Art, scholar Vidya Dehejia describes the Buddha’s enlightenment within a religious path that includes conduct and the rejection of both luxury and severe asceticism.[1] That is specific context. My phrase about a “true spirit” is my own language; it should not be passed off as a definition shared by every Buddhist tradition or every faith.

## Bring the question back into the day

The scene makes a beautiful image. The harder part begins when the image is gone.

One way I can make the question useful is to bring it down to an ordinary choice: a conversation I am avoiding, a mistake I need to acknowledge, a commitment that deserves my attention. These are examples of reflection, not steps that guarantee spiritual awakening.

That is why I connect this post with [individuation and the inner life](/writing/individuation-debugging-the-soul/) and [honesty and keeping promises](/writing/honesty-keeping-promises-and-peace-of-mind/). An idea about who I truly am has to encounter the way I treat people. Otherwise it can remain a beautiful sentence floating above everything I actually do.

You might name the search differently. You might disagree with the premise. The question can still open a useful conversation about what you are living for and what keeps getting in its way.

The water keeps moving under the same moon.

**What is one honest thing you can stop postponing?**
