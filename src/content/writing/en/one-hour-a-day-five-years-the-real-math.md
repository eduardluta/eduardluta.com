“1,826 days” is written across the video. The challenge is direct: can you give yourself five years? Read, practise, develop your work, keep showing up.

I also make numerical claims about becoming better than 90%, 95% and 99.5% of other people, and multiplying a salary tenfold. **Those are unsupported motivational claims, not established results of a five-year formula.** They should not be used as forecasts.

The useful idea is still worth examining: one hour that looks small today can become a substantial amount of practice. We can calculate that without pretending to calculate your future position in the world.

## Does five years equal 1,826 days?

It can. Take a concrete example beginning **26 September 2026** and ending **26 September 2031**. Count the starting date and exclude the ending date, so the last practice day is 25 September 2031. That interval includes 29 February 2028.

Five ordinary years contribute 5 × 365 = 1,825 days. Add that leap day and the total is **1,826 days**. At exactly one hour on each day, that gives **1,826 hours**.

Other five-year intervals can contain a different number of leap days, so 1,826 is not a definition of five years. Dates matter.

Using the same starting date for the intermediate milestones:

| Interval ending | Calendar days | At 1 hour every day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 26 September 2027 | 365 | 365 hours |
| 26 September 2029 | 1,096 | 1,096 hours |
| 26 September 2031 | 1,826 | 1,826 hours |

The table assumes every session happens. If you complete **80% of the planned hours**, the five-year total is 1,826 × 0.8 = **1,460.8 hours**, or 1,460 hours and 48 minutes. This scenario measures time and allows shorter sessions; it is not a count of whole one-hour sessions.

For a log of whole one-hour sessions, count the actual completed sessions instead. You do not need a perfect streak for accumulated effort to exist.

## Hours measure an input

What those hours contain matters. Repeating an easy task and working on a specific weakness are different activities, even if both take an hour.

Research on deliberate practice does not provide a rule that one year puts everyone above 90% of their country, or five years above 99.5% of the world. The Macnamara, Hambrick and Oswald meta-analysis found that practice matters but does not alone explain performance differences; its 2018 correction preserved that broad conclusion. It cannot predict an individual’s salary either.

Before claiming a percentile, you would need a defined skill, a comparison group and an actual performance measure. A calendar provides none of those. Earnings also require their own evidence; they do not follow automatically from a count of study hours.

## Make the next hour accountable

A more useful plan is to name the task you want to perform better, work on a specific part, seek feedback and compare the result with an earlier attempt. Keep a record of what you did and what changed. Adjust when the method is not helping.

That is the commitment I want to carry forward from the clip, alongside the [story about the gifted prince](/writing/gifted-prince-practice-and-the-next-shiny-thing/). Stay with something that matters, but keep checking whether your work is taking you somewhere.

Give the next hour a purpose. Let the result earn its own description.
