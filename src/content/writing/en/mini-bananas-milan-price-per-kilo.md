Mini bananas. Five euros fifty. Milan.

That is almost the whole video: a small banana in my hand, the pack underneath it, and my surprise at the price. The useful detail for understanding the number is on the label: **BANANITO, 250 g**.

**If the €5.50 mentioned in the clip is the price of that 250 g pack, it works out to €22 per kilogram.** The video does not show a receipt or shelf-price label, so that link between price and pack is an assumption, not an independently verified purchase record.

## From €5.50 per pack to a price per kilo

The conversion is straightforward:

- 250 g ÷ 1,000 = 0.25 kg.
- €5.50 ÷ 0.25 kg = **€22/kg**.
- At the same rate, 100 g would correspond to €2.20 and 500 g to €11.

Those last quantities are arithmetic equivalents. They are not additional packs I bought, and they are not offers found in a shop. The calculation uses the labelled pack weight, not a measured weight of peeled fruit.

## Compare the same amount before comparing the price

A package price tells us what that package costs. A price per kilogram lets us compare different pack sizes on the same scale. The EU’s consumer guidance explains unit pricing for precisely this purpose: helping people compare brands and quantities.[1]

For another product, the calculation is the same: **price in euros ÷ weight in kilograms**. If the label uses grams, divide the weight by 1,000 first. Compare €/kg with €/kg; a number marked €/100 g uses a different quantity.

The unit price does not decide whether the taste, size or convenience is worth it to you. It makes the amount of food behind the price clearer.

## What this small purchase cannot tell us

This clip does not establish the average price of bananas in Milan. It also gives us no verified ordinary-banana price from the same shop, so there is no sound basis here for claiming a particular markup. The product name on the pack is not enough to identify a botanical cultivar, either.

The same care with the comparison matters in my [Kosovo–Germany grocery-price article](/writing/kosovo-germany-grocery-prices/): the quantities behind the prices have to be clear before the result means much.

The small banana made the video. The 250 g on the label makes the calculation possible.
