In the video I react angrily to support for flights serving the diaspora. I put it beside everyday problems at home: healthcare, roads and the difficulty of getting institutions to work together.

I also call it vote-buying. That is an accusation in the clip, not a legal finding established by this article. The stronger way to pursue the concern is to read the decision and ask what result the money is meant to deliver.

## What the decision actually says

Government Decision No. 01/21, dated 26 May 2026, allocated **€2 million** under Measure 4.5, support for the diaspora, for air routes serving diaspora destinations. It authorised a cooperation agreement with Pristina airport to design and implement incentives to maintain or add routes during the remaining summer aviation season.[1]

That is a route-support allocation. The decision does not establish a fixed cash payment to every traveller or show that each ticket became cheaper. It also cannot, on its own, establish electoral intent, illegality or an observed benefit.

## Divide the budget, but name the denominator

Suppose the full €2 million were spent. How much would that be per supported **one-way passenger journey**? Without verified participation data, we can only show scenarios:

| Hypothetical supported journeys | Budget divided by journeys |
| ---: | ---: |
| 20,000 | €100 |
| 50,000 | €40 |
| 100,000 | €20 |

The formula is €2,000,000 ÷ journeys. These numbers are neither a promised payment nor a measured ticket discount. A person making a return trip can account for two one-way journeys, so journeys and individual people are not interchangeable.

The exercise shows why an announcement needs an outcome report. A budget total tells me the resources committed. It does not tell me who benefited, whether more seats were offered or what would have happened without support.

## The questions I want answered

I would look for the final scheme terms, selection criteria, amounts actually paid and routes supported. Then I would ask about additional capacity, comparable fares and how the programme assessed its results. Fare comparisons need matching routes, travel dates, baggage and booking lead times; two screenshots of different tickets will not settle the question.

I would also want the public discussion to explain the alternative uses of the money. That does not mean pretending €2 million alone would solve healthcare or infrastructure. It means comparing the expected benefit with other specific, costed options.

That connects with [my question about focusing politics on living standards](/writing/kosovo-politics-focus-on-living-standards/). The diaspora matters. So do the people using services in Kosovo every day. A useful debate can take both seriously.

I can be angry at an announcement. To judge the policy fairly, I need to follow the money through to the result.
