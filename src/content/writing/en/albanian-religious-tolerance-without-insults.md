The message in this video is short: believe what you believe, but do not insult people over it.

I connect that appeal to Albanian religious tolerance and to the importance of staying together. That is the value I am defending. I do not need everybody to arrive at the same answer about God before we can share a community.

**Difference does not have to become contempt.**

## Tolerance still leaves room for disagreement

Respect is sometimes confused with pretending that every belief means the same thing. It does not. People can disagree seriously about scripture, religious institutions, spiritual experiences or the absence of belief.

The question is how we treat the person while discussing the idea. “I do not accept that argument” leaves room for a conversation. An insult usually gives us a different conversation altogether: one about defending ourselves.

## A principle wider than one community

Article 18 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights protects freedom of thought, conscience and religion, including changing a belief. It also recognises expression of religion or belief individually or with others. [¹](#article-notes-heading)

That gives a clear reference point: the freedom I want for myself must also leave room for someone whose conclusion differs from mine.

In the video I describe tolerance as important to Albanian survival. That is a broad historical interpretation, not a claim this short article can prove as a single cause. The practical request stands without reducing a complicated history to one sentence.

## Before posting the reply

Ask whether the reply addresses an idea or attacks the person’s worth. Ask whether you would say it the same way sitting beside them. Then decide what you actually want the conversation to achieve.

These are my suggested questions, not rules that remove anyone’s right to criticise religion. Strong criticism and basic dignity can exist in the same sentence.

Keep your conviction. Leave the insult out.
