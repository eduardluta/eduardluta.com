The Swiss scene in this video comes with a simple thought: imagine the peace of wanting things to be in order. A cared-for space changes what you expect from the next one.

I look at Switzerland and think about what we could build in Kosovo. That is an aspiration, not proof that one country has no problems or that another cannot change. A café is also not a national survey.

What I want to bring home is a more useful question than “why are they better?” **Who keeps the standard alive after the opening day?**

## One example we can actually measure

SBB reported train punctuality of **94.1% in 2025**, compared with **93.2% in 2024**. Its definition counts a train as punctual when the delay is under three minutes. This concerns railway operations, not the café in the clip. [SBB annual punctuality announcement](https://news.sbb.ch/de/019d7b77-a32a-73ef-8a6b-4843122baa27/sbb-so-puenktlich-wie-nie-trotz-vielen-events-und-baustellen) and [measurement explanation](https://company.sbb.ch/en/company/responsibility/customers/punctuality.html).

The improvement is **0.9 percentage points**, not 0.9% of the earlier value. As a simple illustration, applying those rates to 1,000 measured arrivals gives 932 versus 941 punctual arrivals: nine more. The corresponding late share falls from 6.8% to 5.9%, a relative reduction of about **13.2%**. These are scaled examples; they are not actual counts of Swiss trains.

A small movement near a high baseline still requires work. But it does not prove which maintenance action caused the change, and it does not supply a fair comparison with Kosovo's railway without comparable data.

## The part that can travel

I would start with one shared place: a courtyard, a café entrance, a bus stop. Define what “in order” means there. Clear access? Working lights? An emptied bin? Then name who checks it and how a fault gets fixed.

<figcaption>A proposed maintenance loop for one shared place.</figcaption>
</figure>

A photograph records a moment. Maintenance is the series of moments after it. An attractive new bench with no repair plan may tell a different story six months later.

For a small local pilot, keep a dated record of defects reported, defects resolved and days to resolution. Those would be useful measurements. “People here do not care” is not a measurement, and it hides the people already doing the work.

## Admiration should lead to a specific demand

The standard I want is not imported furniture. It is continuity: things remain usable, responsibilities remain clear, and a reported problem reaches someone who can act.

That connects with my [road-safety video](/writing/kosovo-speed-cameras-road-safety/): borrowing an idea means checking how it works. And in [Hamburg](/writing/hamburg-camper-city-connections/), practical infrastructure matters because it lets people get on with the reason they travelled.

A place feels cared for when care becomes repeatable.
